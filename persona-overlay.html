What the manifesto map misses in Bangalore.
Three structural gaps between the archetype framework and what the ethnography actually shows.
Gap 01
"Shalaka is the dominant BLR type — but the brand primarily speaks to Aarav."
9 of 17 respondents are experienced, seasoned pet parents with strong emotional bonds and high knowledge. The brand's primary tone — educational, coaching, reassuring — is calibrated for the first-timer. It's landing on people who don't need to be taught. They need to be respected.
Gap 02
"The immigrant dimension cuts across all 4 archetypes — and the manifesto doesn't name it."
10 of 17 respondents relocated to Bangalore. Aarav immigrants need a local guide. Shalaka immigrants built identity around the pet because the city didn't give them community. Akanksha immigrants research harder because they don't have trusted referrals. Ashok immigrants have systems from their previous city and are rebuilding. Each needs something the original archetype doesn't account for.
Gap 03
"The manifesto has no persona for the welfare-first, high-knowledge, high-conviction parent."
Unit F, Lakshmi, Vihaar, Smrithi — these respondents don't fit cleanly. They're not Aarav (not new). They're not Shalaka (not lonely). They're not Akanksha (not premium-spend driven). They're not Ashok (not control-driven). They are conviction-driven. The manifesto has four quadrants. Bangalore needs a fifth.
