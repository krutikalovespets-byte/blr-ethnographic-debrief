BLR Voice Calibration

What does "friendly neighbourhood pet parent" actually sound like in Bangalore? The manifesto describes the character. This is the field guide.

2a

The Language Reality

These respondents speak Hindi, English, Tamil, Malayalam, and sometimes Kannada. 10 of 17 are from outside Bangalore. The "neighbourhood" is a multilingual, multi-origin, highly mobile one.
Do Don't
Hindi/English — the lingua franca of the immigrant pet parent Kannada-only in any service touchpoint
Acknowledge the chaos of a new city with a new pet Assume they have a network, a trusted vet, a community
Use the specific fears: 10pm pharmacy, Elastic Run, camera guilt Use generic "pet parenting is hard" framing
Name the actual breeds: Shih Tzu, Beagle, Indie, Persian Say "your dog" when you could say "your Shih Tzu"
Reference the immigrant reality: "new to Bangalore? here's what to know" Treat all pet parents as settled Bangaloreans

Product CX

The Conviction Gap

The persona the manifesto doesn't have. The one that's most present in BLR.

5 of 17 respondents are primarily driven by conviction, not comfort. They didn't get a pet on impulse. They adopt Indies from the street. They feed strays. They choose brands that align with their ethics.

They are not the premium Ashok (not control-driven), not the emotional Akanksha (not feel-good driven), not the lonely Shalaka (not warmth-seeking), not the clueless Aarav (not learning). They know what they believe and they're looking for a brand that believes it too.

The Gap

The manifesto's brand purpose: "Because pets and pet parents deserve better." This is the strongest platform for conviction parents. But the brand primarily delivers it through comfort and service.

The conviction parent doesn't want comfort. They want a brand that fights alongside them. The sidekick for this persona isn't helping — it's standing up.

What This Means for the Brand
1

Indie pet content is not a niche — it's a values signal. Treat it as a brand pillar, not a segment. Every time Supertails features an Indie, it recruits the conviction cohort.

2

Welfare messaging needs to be specific, not sentimental. "We donate X% to rescue" beats "we love all animals." The conviction parent has a finely tuned BS detector for performative purpose.

3

The conviction parent is the highest advocacy multiplier in the room. Win them and they recruit. Lose them and they are vocal about why. This cohort does not stay quiet.

Creative Briefs

5 briefs that come directly from the transcripts. Each one is ready to brief an agency or a content team.

Brief 01
"You're watching the camera and feeling worse."
Aarav + Shalaka
Insight Cameras amplify guilt, not security. Parents are more anxious after watching their pet sit alone.
Proposition Name the feeling before selling the solution.
Format Instagram carousel / Reel. No product. Just recognition.
Tone Warm, slightly humorous, deeply human.
"We are always on camera. She is sitting on the bed doing nothing. When we come back, she will not be happy."
— Anushi (G)
Brief 02
"New to Bangalore. One dog. No idea who to call."
Aarav (immigrant variant)
Insight 10 of 17 are immigrants. The arrival moment is the highest-anxiety, lowest-brand-presence moment.
Proposition "Supertails knows Bangalore pet parenting so you don't have to figure it out alone."
Format Welcome kit (email/push) triggered by first order from a new-to-BLR user. City guide: trusted vets by area, breed groups, pet-friendly parks.
Tone Warm, local, practical.
"When I came to Bangalore with two dogs, I had no idea where anything was."
— Lakshmi
Brief 03
"He is my soul partner. I got his name tattooed."
Shalaka (identity-integrated)
Insight For a significant cohort, the pet has restructured their identity. The relationship is not casual.
Proposition Content that honours the depth of the bond — not the products used in service of it.
Format Long-form portrait series — real pet parents, real bonds, no product placement. Brand as witness, not supplier.
Tone Quiet, intimate, Shantell Sans energy.
"He is my soul partner. I got his name tattooed on me."
— Unit I
Brief 04
"The first vet visit defines everything. Forever."
All personas
Insight The first clinic experience creates vet loyalty that is non-transferable.
Proposition Own the first visit. Design it. Name it. Make it a brand moment.
Format Campaign for first clinic visits. "First visit" badge in the app. Post-visit message: "Thank you for trusting us with [pet name]'s first visit."
Tone Capable, warm, ceremonial without being overdone.
"Dr. Prasad came to the house. After that I trust only him."
— Ritik (A)
Brief 05
"Indie parents feed 40 strays. That's not a customer profile. That's a calling."
Akanksha (welfare-mission) + Conviction parents
Insight Welfare-first parents don't want product recommendations. They want a brand that stands for something.
Proposition "Every Supertails order funds [X meals / X vet visits / X rescues]." Specific, verifiable, not sentimental.
Format Transparency report (annual). Monthly counter in the app. Rescue partner stories.
Tone Direct, honest, no fluff.
"We feed about 40 street animals. It is not separate from our life."
— Unit F
